# Word order

The base Mandarin sentence is subject - verb - object, the same order as English. What
differs is where time, place, and adverbs go, and how topics are fronted.

## The base sentence

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 he1 cha2` | I drink tea |
| `ta1 kan4 shu1` | he reads a book |
| `wo3men5 xue2 Zhong1wen2` | we study Chinese |

## Time before the verb

Time expressions come before the verb, not at the end.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 ming2tian1 qu4` | I am going tomorrow |
| `ta1 jin1tian1 xiu1xi5` | he rests today |
| `wo3 yi3hou4 zai4 lai2` | I will come later |

## Place before the verb

Location with `zai4` comes before the verb.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 zai4 jia1 chi1 fan4` | I eat at home |
| `ta1 zai4 yi1yuan4 gong1zuo4` | he works at the hospital |
| `wo3 zai4 zhe4li3 deng3 ni3` | I will wait for you here |

## Adverbs before the verb

Adverbs such as `ye3` (also), `dou1` (all), and `hen3` (very) sit before the verb or
adjective.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 ye3 qu4` | I am going too |
| `wo3men5 dou1 qu4` | we are all going |
| `ta1 hen3 mang2` | he is very busy |

## Adjectives as predicates

An adjective can be the whole predicate. It normally takes `hen3` in a plain statement;
without it the sentence sounds like a comparison. No `shi4` is used.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `zhe4 ge5 cai4 hen3 hao3 chi1` | this dish is tasty |
| `wo3 hen3 lei4` | I am tired |
| `ta1 bu4 mang2` | he is not busy |

Use `shi4` for equivalence, not for adjective predicates.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 shi4 xue2sheng5` | I am a student |
| `zhe4 shi4 wo3 de5 che1` | this is my car |

## Topic-comment

A topic can be fronted and then commented on. The comment is a full clause about it.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `zhe4 ge5 cai4 wo3 xi3huan5` | this dish, I like |
| `Zhong1wen2 wo3 hui4 shuo1 yi1dian3r` | Chinese, I can speak a little |
| `jin1tian1 tian1qi4 hen3 hao3` | today the weather is good |

## The `ba3` construction

`ba3` moves a definite object in front of the verb when the sentence describes what
happens to it. It pairs with a verb that has a result or completion.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 ba3 shu1 kan4 wan2 le5` | I finished reading the book |
| `qing3 ba3 men2 kai1 kai5` | please open the door |
| `ta1 ba3 fan4 chi1 le5` | he ate the meal |

## Authoring notes

- Keep the base order in beginner items; introduce fronted topics and `ba3` only once the
  learner has the plain sentence.
- Put time and place before the verb in generated items too; English-style trailing time
  is the most common authoring slip.
- Use `hen3` with bare adjective predicates, and reserve `shi4` for equivalence.

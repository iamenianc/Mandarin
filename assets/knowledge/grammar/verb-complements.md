# Verb complements

A complement is a word after the verb that completes its meaning: how an action ended, in
which direction it went, whether it can be done, or how well it was done.

## Result complements

| Complement | Pinyin | Verb + complement | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| finish | `wan2` | `kan4wan2` | finish reading / watching |
| done well | `hao3` | `zuo4hao3` | finish doing well |
| reach / manage | `dao4` | `mai3dao4` | manage to buy |
| perceive | `jian4` | `kan4jian4` | see |
| understand | `dong3` | `ting1dong3` | understand by hearing |
| full (eating) | `bao3` | `chi1bao3` | eat one's fill |
| open | `kai1` | `da3kai1` | open up |

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 kan4wan2 le5` | I finished reading |
| `ta1 ting1dong3 le5` | he understood (by ear) |
| `wo3 chi1bao3 le5` | I am full |
| `qing3 da3kai1 men2` | please open the door |

## Direction complements

`lai2` (toward the speaker) and `qu4` (away) combine with direction words.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `jin4lai2` | come in |
| `chu1qu4` | go out |
| `shang4lai2` | come up |
| `xia4qu4` | go down |
| `hui2lai2` | come back |
| `guo4qu4` | go over |
| `qi3lai2` | get up / stand up |

With an object, the object can sit between the two parts: `jin4 wu1zi5 lai2` (come into the
room), where `wu1zi5` is a room.

## Potential complements

Insert `de5` for "can" and `bu4` for "cannot" between the verb and its complement.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `kan4de5jian4` | can see |
| `ting1bu4dong3` | cannot understand by hearing |
| `mai3de5dao4` | can get / can buy |
| `chi1bu4wan2` | cannot finish eating |

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 ting1bu4dong3` | I do not understand (what I hear) |
| `ni3 kan4de5jian4 ma5` | can you see it? |
| `jin1tian1 mai3bu4dao4` | it cannot be bought today |

## Manner complements with `de5`

`de5` after the verb introduces a description of how the action is done.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `ta1 shuo1 de5 hen3 hao3` | he speaks well |
| `ta1 pao3 de5 hen3 kuai4` | he runs fast |
| `ni3 lai2 de5 zhen1 zao3` | you came really early |

Do not confuse this with the potential `de5` above: here `de5` links a manner description,
and the description can be negated (`shuo1 de5 bu4 hao3`).

## The `ba3` construction

`ba3` moves a definite object before a verb that carries a complement. It suits sentences
about a result.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 ba3 shu1 kan4wan2 le5` | I finished reading the book |
| `ta1 ba3 fan4 chi1wan2 le5` | he finished the meal |
| `qing3 ba3 men2 da3kai1` | please open the door |

## The `bei4` passive

`bei4` marks the doer in a passive sentence.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `shu1 bei4 ta1 kan4wan2 le5` | the book was finished by him |
| `wo3 bei4 ren2 kan4jian4 le5` | I was seen |

## Authoring notes

- Keep complements after the verb; the verb is the anchor and the result or direction
  closes it.
- Use `de5` for potential and for manner, and `bu4` inside potential forms to negate.
- Introduce `ba3` and `bei4` after the learner has plain result complements.

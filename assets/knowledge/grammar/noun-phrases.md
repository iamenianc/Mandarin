# Noun phrases

A Mandarin noun phrase puts modifiers before the noun. The particle `de5` links a
descriptive modifier to what it modifies.

## Modifier + `de5` + noun

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 de5 che1` | my car |
| `piao4liang5 de5 hua1` | a beautiful flower |
| `hen3 gui4 de5 fan4dian4` | an expensive restaurant |
| `ta1 mai3 de5 shu1` | the book he bought |

The last example is a relative clause: everything before `de5` describes the noun.

## When `de5` is dropped

Close relationships, some fixed pairs, and single-syllable attributives often do not take
`de5`.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `wo3 ma1ma5` | my mother |
| `Zhong1guo2 ren2` | a Chinese person |
| `lao3 peng2you5` | an old friend |
| `xin1 shou1ji1` | a new phone |

## Demonstrative and number phrases

Pointing and counting use the same frame: demonstrative or number + measure word + noun.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `zhe4 ben3 shu1` | this book |
| `na4 ge5 ren2` | that person |
| `san1 ge5 xue2sheng5` | three students |
| `liang3 bei1 cha2` | two cups of tea |

See `measure-words.md` for choosing the measure word.

## Nominalized phrases

`de5` alone can stand for a noun already known from context.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `zhe4 shi4 wo3 de5` | this is mine |
| `na4 ge5 da4 de5` | the big one |
| `wo3 yao4 hong2 de5` | I want the red one |

## Plurals

Plural `men5` applies to people, not to counted nouns, and never follows a number.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `lao3shi1men5` | the teachers |
| `peng2you5men5` | friends |
| `san1 ge5 lao3shi1` | three teachers |

## Authoring notes

- Keep modifier-before-noun order; English-style trailing clauses are a common slip.
- Choose the measure word for every counted noun; incorrect or missing measure words are a
  frequent generated-content error (see `measure-words.md`).
- Use `de5` for descriptions and possession, and drop it only in the fixed cases above.

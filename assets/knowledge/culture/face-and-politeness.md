# Face and politeness

Face (`mian4zi5`) is the social standing a person holds in a situation. Much politeness
is about protecting it - the other person's and one's own.

## Indirect refusals

A direct "no" can cause loss of face, so refusals are often softened or deferred.

| Pinyin | English | What it can mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ke3neng2 ba5` | maybe | often a soft no |
| `wo3 zai4 xiang3 xiang5` | let me think about it | a polite pause |
| `bu2 tai4 fang1bian4` | not very convenient | a refusal |
| `xia4 ci4 ba5` | maybe next time | a gentle decline |
| `kan4 kan5 zai4 shuo1` | let's see | non-committal |

Understanding this pattern matters for listening: a polite answer may not mean yes.

## Softening requests

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `qing3` | please |
| `ma2fan5 ni3` | sorry to trouble you |
| `bu4 hao3yi4si5` | excuse me / sorry to bother you |
| `ke3yi3 ma5` | would that be all right? |

Adding `yi1dian3r` (a little) or `ke3neng2` (maybe) softens a request or an opinion.

## Compliments and modesty

- A compliment is often deflected with `na3li3 na3li3` (not at all) or a modest comment
  rather than a plain thank you.
- Correcting someone in public, especially an elder, causes loss of face; a correction is
  saved for private.
- When a favor is done, a small token of thanks may follow later, keeping the relationship
  in balance.

## Authoring notes

- Model at least one indirect refusal in conversation drills, and teach the learner to hear
  it as a possible no.
- Keep `na3li3 na3li3` with compliment scenarios; it is a set response, not a real
  question.
- The strength of these conventions varies with closeness and setting; do not treat them as
  universal rules.

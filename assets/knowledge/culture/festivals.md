# Festivals

Major festivals and the greetings that go with them. Several follow the lunar calendar,
so their Gregorian dates move each year.

## Lunar New Year

`chun1jie2` is the most important festival: a family reunion with feasting, red envelopes,
and fireworks. The celebration runs for days around the new year.

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `chun1jie2` | Spring Festival / Lunar New Year |
| `guo4 nian2` | to celebrate the new year |
| `xin1nian2 kuai4le4` | happy new year |
| `gong1xi3 fa1cai2` | may you prosper |
| `hong2bao1` | red envelope |
| `guo4nian2 hao3` | happy new year (greeting) |

## Lantern Festival

`yuan2xiao1jie2` falls on the fifteenth day of the first lunar month and closes the new
year period. Lanterns are displayed and `tang1yuan2` (sweet glutinous rice balls) are
eaten.

## Qingming

`qing1ming2` is a spring festival for tending ancestors' graves. It is a quiet, family
occasion rather than a celebration.

## Dragon Boat Festival

`duan1wu3` falls on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month. Dragon boat races are held and
`zong4zi5` (leaf-wrapped sticky rice) are eaten.

## Mid-Autumn Festival

`zhong1qiu1jie2` centers on the full moon, family reunion, and `yue4bing3` (mooncakes).

## National Day

`guo2qing4` on 1 October marks the founding of the People's Republic and begins a week of
public holidays.

## Authoring notes

- Check the lunar date for the year before scheduling festival content.
- Festival greetings are short set phrases; they suit listening and repeat-after-audio
  drills.
- Keep Qingming low-key; it is not a festive greeting occasion.

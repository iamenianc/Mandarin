# Bargaining and markets

Where haggling is expected, how it is done politely, and where prices are fixed.

## Where prices are negotiable

- **Negotiable:** street markets, tourist stalls, and some independent shops, especially
  for souvenirs and clothing.
- **Fixed:** supermarkets, chain stores, malls, restaurants, and transport. Mobile payment
  and printed prices have made bargaining less common in many places.
- Regional and generational differences are large; follow what nearby customers do.

## How it is done

1. Ask the price: `duo1shao5 qian2`.
2. React with mild surprise: `tai4 gui4 le5` (too expensive).
3. Ask for a lower price: `pian2yi5 yi1dian3r` (a little cheaper).
4. Ask the seller's lowest price: `zui4 di1 duo1shao5`.
5. Walk away only as a last resort, and be willing to be called back.

Keep it friendly and smiling; bargaining is a social exchange, not a confrontation.

## Useful phrases

| Pinyin | English |
| --- | --- |
| `duo1shao5 qian2` | how much? |
| `tai4 gui4 le5` | too expensive |
| `pian2yi5 yi1dian3r` | a little cheaper |
| `zui4 di1 duo1shao5` | what is your lowest price? |
| `wo3 zai4 kan4 yi1 kan5` | I will look around a bit more |
| `hao3, wo3 mai3 le5` | all right, I will buy it |

## Paying

- Cash is accepted, but mobile payment by QR code is the everyday method in cities.
- Ask for a receipt only at larger shops; small stalls often do not provide one.
- Do not expect change to be given cheerfully for very large notes; use small money.

## Authoring notes

- Keep bargaining scenarios at a market stall, where they are appropriate.
- Pair `tai4 gui4 le5` with `pian2yi5 yi1dian3r` as a two-turn exchange.
- Note that haggling is not appropriate in fixed-price settings; the field-mission persona
  should not invite it there.

# Spelling conventions

Pinyin spelling is not a one-to-one letter-to-sound code. Several finals are abbreviated,
the umlaut is dropped in some contexts, and zero-initial syllables gain a `y` or `w`.

## Abbreviated finals

| Written | Full form | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `iu` | `iou` | `liu4` | six |
| `ui` | `uei` | `shui3` | water |
| `un` | `uen` | `cun1` | village |

These shortcuts apply after an initial. With a zero initial the fuller spelling shows:
`you3` (to have), `wei4` (for), `wen4` (to ask).

## The umlaut

The vowel `ü` is written `u` after `j`, `q`, `x`, and `y`:

| Written | Spoken vowel | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ju` | jü | `ju4` | sentence |
| `que` | qüe | `que4` | but |
| `xu` | xü | `xu1` | to need |
| `yu` | ü | `yu2` | fish |

Elsewhere the umlaut is kept: `lü4` (green), `nü3` (woman). This is why `lu4` (road) and
`lü4` (green) are different words, and why a `j` / `q` / `x` syllable with `u` is never
pronounced like `gu` or `ku`.

## Zero-initial syllables

When a syllable has no initial, its first vowel is respelled with `y` or `w`.

| Final | Written | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `i` | `yi` | `yi1` | one |
| `ie` | `ye` | `ye3` | also |
| `iao` | `yao` | `yao4` | to want |
| `iou` | `you` | `you3` | to have |
| `ian` | `yan` | `yan3` | eye |
| `in` | `yin` | `yin1` | sound |
| `iang` | `yang` | `yang2` | sheep |
| `ing` | `ying` | `ying1` | hero |
| `iong` | `yong` | `yong4` | to use |
| `u` | `wu` | `wu3` | five |
| `ua` | `wa` | `wa1` | tile |
| `uo` | `wo` | `wo3` | I |
| `uai` | `wai` | `wai4` | outside |
| `uei` | `wei` | `wei4` | for |
| `uan` | `wan` | `wan2` | to finish |
| `uen` | `wen` | `wen4` | to ask |
| `uang` | `wang` | `wang2` | king |
| `ueng` | `weng` | `weng1` | old man |
| `ü` | `yu` | `yu2` | fish |
| `üe` | `yue` | `yue4` | month |
| `üan` | `yuan` | `yuan2` | round |
| `ün` | `yun` | `yun2` | cloud |

`y` and `w` add no consonant sound; they mark a syllable boundary in writing.

## Syllable boundaries

When a syllable beginning with `a`, `o`, or `e` follows another syllable, an apostrophe
marks the boundary: `Xi1'an1` (a place name, the only common case). Without the mark,
adjacent vowels could be read as one final.

## Authoring notes

- Store pinyin in the written form (`liu2`, `shui3`, `ju4`), not the underlying form.
- Because `u` after `j` / `q` / `x` / `y` means `ü`, read those syllables with the rounded
  front vowel; feedback should name the final accordingly.
- All stored pinyin carries tone numbers (ADR 0012); spelling conventions never replace a
  tone number.

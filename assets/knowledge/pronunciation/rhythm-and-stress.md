# Rhythm and stress

Mandarin is syllable-timed: each syllable takes roughly the same amount of time, and the
tone on that syllable carries most of the pitch information. Stress exists, but it is
lighter than in English and does not change vowel quality the way English reduces
unstressed vowels to a schwa.

## What shapes rhythm

- **Syllable timing.** Keep syllables even; do not compress unstressed syllables into a
  mumble. `xie4xie5` is two clear syllables, not "shyeh".
- **Tone as the melody.** Pitch movement comes from the tones, not from sentence-level
  stress. A rising sentence intonation sits on top of each syllable's own contour.
- **Neutral tone as weak position.** Neutral-tone syllables are short and light, but their
  vowel is still identifiable: `de5`, `le5`, `ma5`, `zi5`.
- **Grouping.** Speakers group syllables into rhythm units with small pauses and
  boundary tone adjustments, which is where third-tone sandhi applies (see
  `tone-sandhi.md`).

## Common function words that are usually neutral

| Pinyin | Role | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `de5` | possessive / modifier particle | `wo3 de5 shu1` (my book) |
| `le5` | completed action / change | `ta1 zou3 le5` (he left) |
| `ma5` | yes / no question | `ni3 hao3 ma5` (how are you?) |
| `ne5` | follow-up question | `wo3 ne5` (and me?) |
| `ba5` | suggestion / assumption | `zou3 ba5` (let's go) |
| `men5` | plural marker for people | `wo3men5` (we) |
| `zi5` | noun suffix | `zhuo1zi5` (table) |
| `ge5` | second half of many two-syllable words | `ge1ge5` (elder brother) |

## Reduplication

Repeating a syllable often neutralizes the second copy:

| Written | Speech | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ma1 ma5` | ma1 ma5 | mother |
| `di4 di5` | di4 di5 | younger brother |
| `kan4 kan5` | kan4 kan5 | take a look |
| `shi4 shi5` | shi4 shi5 | give it a try |

## Authoring notes

- Keep reference audio syllable-clear; the learner is imitating rhythm as much as tones.
- Do not mark stress in pinyin. If a lesson needs to point at prominence, describe it in
  words ("the second syllable is light").
- Watch for English habits in feedback: added stress on content words, and dropped final
  syllables in multi-syllable words.

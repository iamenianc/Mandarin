# Tone contours

Mandarin has four full tones plus a neutral tone. Tones are carried on the vowel of a
syllable and are the main source of meaning difference between otherwise identical
syllables.

| Tone | Number | Contour | Chao value | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | `1` | high level | 55 | steady high pitch, no movement |
| Second | `2` | rising | 35 | starts mid, rises to high |
| Third | `3` | low dipping | 214 | starts low, dips, then rises; often creaky |
| Fourth | `4` | falling | 51 | starts high, falls sharply |
| Neutral | `5` | light | varies | short and weak; pitch follows the preceding tone |

The Chao values describe relative pitch, not absolute frequency: they are a shape to
imitate, not a note to hit.

## What each tone does

- **Tone 1 (high level).** A long, even, high pitch. Common error: drifting down, which
  sounds like tone 4.
- **Tone 2 (rising).** A clean upward glide across the syllable. Common error: a shallow
  rise or a dip first, which sounds like tone 3.
- **Tone 3 (low dipping).** The pitch drops low and stays low; the final rise is often
  incomplete in speech. Common error: rising early, which sounds like tone 2. Tone 3
  before another tone is usually a "half third" (low falling, written `3`) - see
  `tone-sandhi.md`.
- **Tone 4 (falling).** A sharp, complete drop from high to low. Common error: not
  falling far enough, which blurs into tone 1 or the neutral tone.
- **Neutral tone (5).** Short and unstressed, with pitch shaped by the preceding syllable
  (lower after tone 3, higher after tone 1). It is never a full contour.

## Practice set

The classic five-way set uses one syllable across all tones:

| Pinyin | Tone | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ma1` | 1 | mother |
| `ma2` | 2 | hemp |
| `ma3` | 3 | horse |
| `ma4` | 4 | scold |
| `ma5` | 5 | question particle |

More single-syllable sets for tone naming and production drills:

| Pinyin | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `shi1` | poem |
| `shi2` | ten |
| `shi3` | history |
| `shi4` | is / matter |
| `ji1` | chicken |
| `ji2` | extreme |
| `ji3` | how many |
| `ji4` | to remember |

Tones are taught first, in a dedicated module ahead of vocabulary (ADR 0012), and named
by number in feedback.

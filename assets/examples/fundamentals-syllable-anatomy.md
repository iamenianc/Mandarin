# Syllable anatomy

A Mandarin syllable is an initial followed by a final, plus a tone. The examples
below show the split for sounds a beginner meets early (ADR 0008, ADR 0012).

| pinyin | initial | final | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ma1` | m | a | mother |
| `hao3` | h | ao | good |
| `ni3` | n | i | you |
| `shui3` | sh | ui | water |
| `ren2` | r | en | person |
| `zhong1` | zh | ong | middle |
| `guo2` | g | uo | country |
| `xie4` | x | ie | thank |
| `liu4` | l | iu | six |
| `wang3` | w | ang | toward |

Some syllables have no initial, such as `an1` (peace) and `er4` (two). The
spellings `iu`, `ui`, and `un` are short forms of `iou`, `uei`, and `uen`; the
app shows the short form and the audio carries the sound.
